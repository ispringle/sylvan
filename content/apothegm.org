#+TITLE: Apothegm

/Apothegm/ is a funny word you might use to let others know you are infinitely
smarter than they are. It's a funny word with some serious clout, but ultimately
it just means "a terse, witty, instructive saying; a maxim." I found the word
while trying to find a better term than "phrase". There's actually a number of
words which would have worked, /apothegm/ is just the funniest of them. I could
have said /sententia/, /aphorism/, /epigram/, /maxim/, /adage/, /byword/, /proverb/, or even
the never used definition of /saw/.

Anywho[fn:: The use of /anywho/ really detracts from the air of genius I was
cultivating when I whipped out "apothegm"], these are a number of /apothegms/ that
I've found enjoyable.

* Latin
These are Latin sayings and phrases I like or find contemplative.
** /pax vobiscum/
In English /pax vobiscum/ means "peace [be] with you". It would have been a fairly
common salutation in Roman times. It also is used in some liturgical functions
today, [[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pax_(liturgy)#Liturgical_use][particularly in Greek Orthodox churches]].

The word /pax/ is probably a fairly well recognized Latin word and it means
"peace". That then means /vobiscum/ is the "[be] with you" portion of this
salutation.
** /vincit qui se vincit/
In English this means "he conquers who conquers himself". Sometimes the phrase
is prepended with the word /bis/ which translates to the much wordier, "he who
prevails over himself is twice victorious". This is a more famous Latin phrase
as it is featured in the movie /Beauty and The Beast/.
